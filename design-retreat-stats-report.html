What this means

The headline is encouraging: your average heart rate dropped nearly 13 bpm — likely driven by breathwork, reduced caffeine, movement, and removal from daily stressors. The nuance is in nighttime metrics. Sleeping HR and lowest HR ticked upward while HRV dipped slightly. This is consistent with a body in active recovery — intensive retreat activities (healing ceremonies, emotional workshops, spa therapies) can temporarily elevate nighttime cardiovascular activity as the body processes stimuli. This is your body healing, not struggling. Research on meditation retreats shows HRV often dips in week one before rebounding higher than baseline in weeks two and three.

Key insights

Connecting the dots

🌙 REM sleep saw the largest positive shift

+22.53% (+16.64 min/night). REM is critical for emotional regulation and memory consolidation. Why it likely happened: retreat environments reduce the two biggest REM suppressors — alcohol and late-night screens. Combined with physically active days and intentional wind-down rituals, your brain had ideal conditions to prioritize REM cycles.

❤️ Average heart rate dropped substantially

-12.76% (-12.54 bpm). Why it likely happened: the retreat removed chronic stressors (work, commuting, connectivity) and replaced them with parasympathetic-activating practices. A drop this large in 7 days suggests your baseline was elevated by cumulative stress, and the retreat gave your nervous system permission to stand down.

⏰ You spent 14% less time awake at night

-14.06% (-13.24 min). Why it likely happened: nighttime wakefulness correlates with cognitive arousal — the "racing mind" effect. Physical exhaustion from movement, emotional release from ceremonies, and no late-night scrolling allowed your nervous system to sustain sleep. Less fragmentation also explains the REM gain — uninterrupted cycles let the brain reach later-stage REM more consistently.

💓 HRV dipped slightly — and that is okay

-3.91% (-0.57 ms). Why it likely happened: HRV is highly sensitive to acute physical and emotional load. Cold plunge, emotional workshops, new movement patterns, and altitude changes all place short-term demands on the autonomic nervous system. The small magnitude suggests a transient adaptation, not a concern. A follow-up 1–2 weeks post-retreat would likely show HRV rebounding above baseline.

📊 A note on statistical significance

None of the metrics reached statistical significance — expected with a 7-day window and individual-level data. The trends are directionally promising and internally consistent. A longer study or larger group would provide the power to confirm these patterns.
