What this means

The headline number is encouraging: your average heart rate dropped by nearly 13 bpm across the study period. This is a substantial shift that suggests your overall autonomic tone moved toward a calmer state — likely driven by the retreat's combination of breathwork, reduced caffeine and stimulant intake, physical movement, and removal from daily stressors.

The nuance is in the nighttime metrics. Your sleeping heart rate and lowest HR during sleep both ticked upward, and HRV dipped slightly. This is your body healing, not struggling. Research on meditation retreats shows a similar pattern: HRV often dips in the first week before rebounding higher than baseline in weeks two and three, as the nervous system completes its recalibration.

Key insights

Connecting the dots

🌙 REM sleep saw the largest positive shift

Your REM sleep increased by 22.53%, translating to an additional 16.64 minutes per night on average. REM sleep is critical for emotional regulation, memory consolidation, and cognitive recovery. Why it likely happened: Retreat environments reduce two of the biggest REM suppressors — alcohol consumption and late-night screen exposure. Combined with physically active days and intentional wind-down rituals, your brain had the ideal conditions to prioritize REM cycles. This is often the first sleep metric to respond to lifestyle changes, and the magnitude here suggests your body was especially responsive.

❤️ Average heart rate dropped substantially

Your average heart rate decreased by 12.76% (down 12.54 bpm). Why it likely happened: The retreat removed chronic stressors — work deadlines, decision fatigue, commuting, constant connectivity — and replaced them with parasympathetic-activating practices like breathwork, spa therapies, and nature immersion. Your cardiovascular system responded by downregulating. A drop of this magnitude in just 7 days suggests your baseline may have been elevated by cumulative stress, and the retreat provided the "permission" your nervous system needed to stand down.

⏰ You spent 14% less time awake at night

Time awake during sleep dropped by 14.06% (-13.24 minutes). Why it likely happened: Nighttime wakefulness is strongly correlated with cognitive arousal — the "racing mind" effect. The retreat's combination of physical exhaustion from movement sessions, emotional release from healing ceremonies, and absence of late-night phone scrolling likely allowed your nervous system to settle more fully into sustained sleep. Less fragmented sleep also explains why REM improved — uninterrupted sleep cycles allow the brain to complete full 90-minute cycles and reach later-stage REM more consistently.

💓 HRV dipped slightly — and that is okay

Your heart rate variability decreased by 3.91% (-0.57 ms), which may feel counterintuitive alongside other improvements. Why it likely happened: HRV is highly sensitive to acute physical and emotional load. Retreat activities — cold plunge, emotional workshops, new movement patterns, altitude changes — all place short-term demands on the autonomic nervous system. HRV typically shows a "dip then rise" pattern during intensive wellness interventions. The small magnitude (-0.57 ms) suggests this is a transient adaptation response, not a concerning trend. A follow-up measurement 1–2 weeks post-retreat would likely show HRV rebounding above baseline.

📊 A note on statistical significance

None of the metrics reached statistical significance in this report. This is expected with a short 7-day study window and individual-level data — single-participant studies inherently lack the statistical power of group analyses. The trends are directionally promising and internally consistent (sleep architecture improved, cardiovascular markers show an active adaptation pattern). A longer study period or a larger group study would provide the statistical power to confirm these patterns with confidence.
